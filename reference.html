My pricing formula

My floor cost = materials per piece + (my hourly rate × hours per piece) + overhead per piece
My launch price = floor × 1.30 (minimum) to floor × 1.50 (maximum)
My print price increase = +10–15% on copies 6–10 once the first 5 sell — two bands per edition, simple to track
My originals price increase = +10–15% after every 4–6 originals of a given type sell
I never price anything below my floor. I track sales counts per edition and per surface type to apply increases on schedule.

My progressive pricing rule — prints and originals only

↑
I raise my prices as my work sells
Applies to all my prints and originals · my membership pricing is fixed and excluded
Prints — edition of 10

Copies 1–5: launch price
Copies 6–10: +10–15% on launch price

One trigger per edition. When copy 5 sells, I update the listing price immediately before copy 6 goes.

Originals — per surface type

Sales 1–5: launch price
Sales 6+: +10–15% on current price

I track by surface type (e.g. all 8×10" linen). After 4–6 of that type sell, I raise the price on the next piece of the same type.

How I apply this
For prints, it's two prices per edition — that's it. Launch price for copies 1–5, increased price for copies 6–10. I note both prices when I list the edition so I only have to update once mid-run.

I compound on my current price, not my launch price. The increase applies to whatever the price is now. I round to the nearest $5 for clean listings.

I announce it in my shop. "Edition of 10 — price increases at copy 6" tells buyers exactly where they stand and gives them a genuine reason to act without any pressure from me.

When an edition closes, I move on to the next work. The sold-out edition stays visible on my site marked as sold out — it builds my track record and signals demand to new visitors.

My channel adjustments

Direct (Shopify / my website): I use my prices as listed above.
Gallery or shop (40–50% commission): I gross up my price so my net is always protected.
My gallery list price = my target net ÷ (1 − commission rate)
Example: I want to net $300 → I list at $545 for a 45% commission gallery. My progressive increases still apply to the gallery list price.
I keep my direct and gallery list prices publicly consistent. If a gallery lists a piece at $545, I match that price on my website — or I negotiate exclusivity terms with the gallery.

My member discount (when I'm ready to launch it)

I plan to offer 10% off prints and originals for active postcard club members, applied at checkout via discount code. The discount applies to my current band price — so members buying copies 6–10 of a print pay 10% less than the increased public price, not 10% less than the launch price.

All prices in CAD. My floors use midpoint estimates: $375 materials, $62/hr labour, $500+ studio overhead ÷ 6 pieces/month.

My print launch prices reflect an edition of 10 — genuinely scarce, so I launch higher than I would for a larger edition. One price increase per edition at copy 6.

My $5 finishing cost per print is included in my Tier 2 floor costs. Finishing for originals (box, corner protectors, foam) to be calculated separately.

I refine my floor costs as I track my actual hours per piece and actual materials spend each month.
